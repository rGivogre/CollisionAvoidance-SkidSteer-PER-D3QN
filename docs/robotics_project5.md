# ROBOTICS PROJECTS - 2025/2026

The robotics project is an optional activity designed to allow students to explore and understand how the theoretical concepts covered in the course find concrete application in real-world scenarios. This year, the projects will follow a research-oriented approach.

The following pages describe 13 possible projects, each of which is related to one of the topics covered in class. A reference paper is provided for each project. In some cases, **additional articles may also be included** as a starting point for exploring new robotics topics. Please note that while these supplementary references are helpful, they may not be sufficient for a deep dive into the advanced concepts of the chosen work. Students will work in groups of up to 3-5 people, with the option of choosing only one project per group. The focus will be on studying the reference paper to understand the goals of the study and the proposed improvements, investigating unfamiliar concepts through research, implementing the work, and attempting to replicate the reported results.

Some of the proposed projects have a higher level of difficulty and therefore require additional effort, both in terms of time and in the study of additional topics not covered during the semester. Since exactly replicating the results of the paper using the same tools, software and equipment can be complex, modifications or simplifications about the methodology considered in the reference papers are allowed if reasonable and justified.

In addition, some of the papers propose real-world experiments. For the purposes of the project, it is not necessary to perform real-world validation. For all proposed projects, the implementation and validation of the results can be performed in a simulative environment using any tool, software, or programming language of your choice.

The project delivery will include:

- A report (pdf) of maximum 3 pages (written in Overleaf/LaTeX or Word), including the analysis of the addressed problem, the methodology and considerations made during implementation phase, the main results obtained from simulations and possible extensions of the project;
- A power point presentation of the work;
- a **7-minute video**, which will be published on the course website;

The evaluation of the project will be based on:

- Understanding of the original material: **2 points**
- Implementation and innovation: **3 points**
- Quality of presentation: **1 point**
- The contribution given by each member of the group to the realization and implementation of the project (group members who do not contribute will receive a final score of 0 points)

## 5. Lidar-Based Deep Reinforcement Learning for Collision Avoidance in Maze-Like Environments

Navigating unknown environments, especially in compact spaces such as narrow corridors, presents significant challenges for autonomous robots. Traditional methods, like potential field-based approaches and dynamic window methods, often struggle to efficiently avoid collisions in such settings. Deep reinforcement learning (DRL) has emerged as a promising alternative for solving complex collision avoidance problems, as it enables robots to learn optimal navigation strategies through trial and error. In \[1\], authors implement a lidar-based deep reinforcement learning method for collision avoidance in maze-like environments.

**Paper:**

\[1\] Feng Shumin et al., "A Collision Avoidance Method Based on Deep Reinforcement Learning"

**Additional references:**

\[2\] Murphy P. Kevin, "Reinforcement Learning: An Overview"  
\[3\] Singh Barat et al., "Reinforcement learning in robotic applications: a comprehensive survey"  
\[4\] Tang Cheng et al., "Deep Reinforcement Learning for Robotics: A Survey of Real-World Successes"
